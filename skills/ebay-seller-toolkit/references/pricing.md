# eBay Pricing Research Reference

## Pricing Research Methodology

### Step 1: Search Sold Listings

1. Go to eBay Advanced Search or use the search bar
2. Enter the exact product name/model number
3. Check "Sold Items" under "Show only"
4. Filter by condition matching the item being priced
5. Sort by "Date: Newest First" to see recent sales

### Step 2: Analyze the Data

- **Sample size**: Aim for at least 10-20 sold listings for reliable data
- **Time range**: Focus on sales from the last 30-90 days
- **Remove outliers**: Ignore abnormally high/low prices (damaged items, bundle deals, bidding wars)
- **Note patterns**: Identify if prices are trending up, down, or stable

### Step 3: Calculate Price Range

```
Competitive Price = Median of recent sold prices (adjusted for condition)
Floor Price = 25th percentile of sold prices
Ceiling Price = 75th percentile of sold prices
```

### Step 4: Adjust for Condition

| Condition | Price Adjustment |
|-----------|-----------------|
| New/Sealed | Base price (100%) |
| Open Box / Like New | 80-90% of new |
| Very Good | 65-80% of new |
| Good | 50-65% of new |
| Acceptable / For Parts | 20-40% of new |

### Step 5: Factor in Extras

- Original box/packaging: +5-15%
- Complete accessories: +10-20%
- Extended warranty remaining: +5-10%
- Rare color/variant: +10-50%
- Celebrity provenance / signed: highly variable

## Pricing Strategies

### Competitive Pricing

Set price at or slightly below the median of recent sold listings. Best for:
- Common items with high supply
- Quick turnover goals
- Building seller reputation

### Premium Pricing

Set price 10-20% above median. Justified when:
- Item condition is significantly better than average
- Includes extra accessories/warranties
- Professional photos and detailed description
- Strong seller feedback (1000+ with 99%+ positive)

### Penetration Pricing

Set price 10-20% below median. Use when:
- New seller account building feedback
- Need quick cash flow
- Item has been listed for 30+ days without selling

### Dynamic Pricing

- Relist every 7-14 days at a lower price if no sale
- Typical markdown schedule: Week 1 (100%) → Week 3 (95%) → Week 5 (90%) → Week 7 (85%)
- Set a minimum acceptable price before listing

## Common Pricing Mistakes

1. **Pricing from active listings** — Unsold items tell you what the market WON'T pay
2. **Ignoring shipping in price comparison** — Compare total cost (item + shipping) not just item price
3. **Emotional pricing** — "I paid $X for it" is irrelevant; market value is what matters
4. **Not accounting for eBay fees** — 15-17% of sale price goes to fees
5. **Seasonal blindness** — Some items have strong seasonal demand patterns (e.g., heaters in winter, AC units in summer)

## Seasonal Pricing Considerations

| Season | Higher Demand | Lower Demand |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| Q1 (Jan-Mar) | Fitness equipment, tax software, organization | Holiday decorations, summer items |
| Q2 (Apr-Jun) | Outdoor/garden, summer clothing, sports | Winter clothing, heaters |
| Q3 (Jul-Sep) | Back-to-school, electronics, fall fashion | Tax software, spring items |
| Q4 (Oct-Dec) | Gifts, toys, electronics, holiday items | Fitness, spring/summer |

## Currency & International Pricing

- eBay Global Shipping Program handles customs for sellers
- Price in listing currency; international buyers see converted prices
- Consider exchange rate fluctuations for high-value items
- International listings can expand buyer pool by 20-40%
